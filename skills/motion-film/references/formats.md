# Formats and script patterns

## Advert (6, 15 or 30 s)

| Share of length | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0 to 10% | Hook: the problem or the desire, in a picture, in the first second |
| 10 to 60% | The product doing one thing, shown not described |
| 60 to 80% | The benefit, in five words or fewer |
| 80 to 100% | Logo, call to action, URL, held at least 2 s |

One product, one benefit, one action. Works muted: the text on screen carries the message.

## Marketing film (30 to 60 s)

Situation, turn, resolution.

1. A person and a specific situation (a seller who just won a large order).
2. The friction, concrete and small (paid in 60 days, supplier wants paying now).
3. The product enters at the turn and changes one thing.
4. The new situation, shown.
5. Close: name, line, call to action.

Open on a line a person would say, not a slogan: "I need forty solar lamps for my shop. Who can deliver them?"

## Educational explainer (60 to 180 s)

- Hook (5 to 10 s): a real question or a quoted real post.
- Numbered chapters (01 The problem, 02 The gap ...). Each opens with its title for 1 s.
- One running example with consistent figures across chapters, labelled as illustrative.
- Diagrams where every line is a request, a payment or a permission. No decorative connectors.
- Recap: the whole loop in one frame.
- Close.

## Tutorial

Real captures for actions that need proof (a click that does something), authored scenes for ideas a recording cannot show. Zoom into the part of the screen that matters; never show a full desktop at 1080p and expect it to be read.

## Direction template

```
Title:
Opening line:
| Time | Picture and motion | Spoken idea or text |
Motion character:
Truth boundary:
Why this one:
```

## Writing for the screen

- One idea per line; lines under 9 words on screen.
- Specific nouns and numbers ("12 rolls of branded tape, Lagos delivery") beat categories ("physical goods").
- No hype words (revolutionary, seamless, game-changing). If the picture shows it, the text does not need to say it.
- Label honestly and visibly: "Illustration", "Example figures", "Planned", "Testnet". A label the viewer cannot read does not count.
